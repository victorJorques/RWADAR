I. Crypto-linked Expiry Futures

Crypto-linked Expiry Futures are cash-settled derivative contracts that provide leveraged
exposure to the price movements of underlying crypto-assets, with a fixed settlement date.
Crypto-linked Expiry Futures are complex financial instruments. You should only trade
Crypto-linked Expiry Futures if You fully understand leverage, margin requirements, liquidation
procedures, the settlement mechanism, and the specific risks associated with crypto-asset
derivatives. As these contracts involve leverage, they are subject to margin requirements, which
means that You can gain or lose significantly more than Your initial investment. Retail clients are
subject to negative balance protection, meaning retail clients may never lose more than the
assets in their Trading Account, including assets placed as collateral for the leveraged trade.
Non-retail clients may, in exceptional circumstances, incur losses beyond their initial investment.

receive a margin call requiring additional funds or collateral. Failure to meet margin
requirements will result in forced liquidation of Your positions, potentially at unfavourable prices.
During volatile markets, liquidations can occur rapidly with little or no notice. You should ensure
You understand the settlement mechanism and have adequate resources to fulfil Your
obligations before placing a trade.

II. TradFi Expiry Futures - Equity Single Stock and ETF Futures

Equity single stock and ETF expiry futures are cash-settled derivative contracts that provide

      trading halts affecting the ETF itself, may affect the settlement value of Your position.

      affecting constituents of the reference security or the constituent securities of the

III. TradFi Expiry Futures - Commodity Futures

Commodity expiry futures are cash-settled derivative contracts that provide leveraged exposure
to the price of physical commodities (including but not limited to gold, silver, crude oil, and
natural gas), with a fixed settlement date and maturities of up to five (5) years. These are
complex financial instruments. Before trading, You should understand the following specific
risks:

   ●​ Supply and demand risk: Commodity prices are determined by global supply and
      demand dynamics, which are in turn affected by geopolitical events, weather patterns,
      production decisions (including by state actors and bodies such as OPEC), and
      technological changes. These factors can cause extreme price volatility and may be
      difficult to predict.
   ●​ Geopolitical and macroeconomic risk: Commodity prices are particularly sensitive to
      international political instability, sanctions, trade restrictions, and macroeconomic
      conditions. Events in producing or consuming countries can cause sudden and severe
      price movements.
   ●​ Basis risk: The price of a commodity futures contract may differ materially from the
      price of the underlying physical commodity. This basis can fluctuate significantly and
      independently of movements in the commodity spot price.
   ●​ Funding rate risk: Holding a position in commodity futures is subject to a periodic
      funding rate, under which payments are exchanged between long and short position
      holders based on the difference between the contract price and the index price. The
      funding rate may be positive or negative and can vary materially with market conditions.
      Funding payments may represent a significant cumulative cost or income over time,
      particularly for positions held over extended periods.
   ●​ Roll yield and term structure risk: The futures curve for commodities may be in
      contango (futures price higher than spot) or backwardation (futures price lower than
      spot). Rolling positions from one contract to the next may result in a roll cost (in
      contango) or roll gain (in backwardation). Over time, particularly for longer-dated
      contracts, these roll dynamics can materially affect returns.
   ●​ Position limits: Trading in commodity derivatives is subject to position limits under
      Article 57 of Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II) and applicable implementing measures.

      applicable position limit requirements. Exceeding position limits may result in

IV. Options

Options are cash-settled option contracts referencing crypto-assets or commodities, including
gold. They grant the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to receive a cash payment based on
the difference between the settlement price of the relevant underlying asset or reference index
and the agreed strike price at the scheduled expiry time. The settlement value is determined by
reference to the applicable index price at expiry. These are complex financial instruments. You
should only trade Options if You fully understand the option premium, exercise mechanics,
settlement methodology, margin requirements for option sellers, and the specific risks described
below:

   ●​ Premium loss risk: As a buyer of an Option, the maximum loss is limited to the
      premium paid. If the relevant underlying price does not reach the strike price (for a call
      option) or fall below the strike price (for a put option) by the scheduled expiry, the option
      will expire worthless and You will lose the entire premium paid.

   ●​ Unlimited loss risk for option sellers: As a seller of a call option, Your potential loss is
      theoretically unlimited, as there is no ceiling on how far the relevant underlying price may
      rise. As a seller of a put option, the maximum loss is equal to the strike price multiplied
      by the contract size (i.e., if the underlying falls to zero). You should fully understand
      these risks before selling any option.

   ●​ Margin requirements for option sellers: Selling options requires You to post and
      maintain margin throughout the life of the contract. If the relevant underlying price moves
      against Your sold position and Your Account margin falls below required levels, You will
      receive a margin call requiring additional funds or collateral. Failure to meet margin
      requirements will result in forced liquidation of Your position, potentially at unfavourable
      prices.

  ●​ Time decay (theta) risk: The time value component of an option premium decreases as
     the expiry date approaches, a phenomenon known as time decay. For option buyers,
     time decay represents a continuous cost, independent of movements in the relevant
     underlying price. Options that are out-of-the-money will lose their entire time value if the
     underlying does not move sufficiently before expiry.

  ●​ Implied volatility risk: Option premiums are highly sensitive to changes in implied
     volatility. A decrease in implied volatility will reduce the value of a long option position
     even if the relevant underlying price moves in the expected direction. Implied volatility in
     both crypto-asset and commodity markets can be extreme and unpredictable, and You
     may incur losses attributable solely to volatility changes rather than directional price
     movements.

  ●​ Delta and non-linear risk: Unlike futures contracts, which have a linear payoff, options
     have a non-linear relationship between the option premium and the relevant underlying
     price. The sensitivity of an option's value to movements in the underlying (delta) changes
     dynamically as the underlying price moves relative to the strike. Gamma measures the
     rate at which delta changes as the underlying price changes. Gamma can become
     particularly significant for options that are near the strike price and close to expiry,
     meaning that small movements in the underlying price may cause rapid and
     disproportionate changes in the value and risk profile of an Option. This non-linearity
     means that Your position's profit and loss profile may change rapidly and in complex
     ways, particularly as the option approaches expiry or moves significantly in- or
     out-of-the-money.

  ●​ Settlement and expiry risk: At the scheduled expiry time, Options are automatically
     cash-settled by reference to the applicable settlement index price, determined in
     accordance with the relevant product rules and index methodology. You cannot exercise
     early. Any disruption to the index price calculation at or around expiry may affect
     settlement. If You do not close Your position before expiry, it will be settled automatically;
     You should understand the settlement mechanics fully before trading.

  ●​ Interaction with underlying asset risks: The risks applicable to the relevant underlying
     asset also apply to Options. Crypto-asset-referencing Options are exposed to
     technology, cybersecurity, extreme volatility, regulatory, market-manipulation and liquidity
     risks. Commodity-referencing Options, including options referencing gold, are exposed
     to supply-and-demand, geopolitical, market-hours, price-gap and benchmark risks.
     Because options are sensitive to the underlying price, volatility and time, adverse
     developments in these factors may affect Your position before expiry.

  ●​ Liquidity risk for options: Option markets may have lower liquidity than equivalent
     futures or spot markets, particularly for out-of-the-money options or options with longer
RISK WARNING & DISCLOSURE S​​TATEMENT




       maturities. Wide bid-ask spreads may make it difficult to enter or exit positions at desired
       prices, and the cost of unwinding a position may be significant.

   ●​ Leverage: Options provide implicit leverage. A relatively small movement in the relevant
      underlying price can result in a disproportionately large percentage change in the value
      of the option premium. While the maximum loss for a buyer is the premium paid, the
      effective leverage embedded in an out-of-the-money option can be very high. Option
      sellers are subject to the full leverage risk of an open derivative position without the
      premium protection available to buyers.

You should ensure You understand all of the above risks and all applicable margin requirements
and settlement procedures before placing a trade in Options.
