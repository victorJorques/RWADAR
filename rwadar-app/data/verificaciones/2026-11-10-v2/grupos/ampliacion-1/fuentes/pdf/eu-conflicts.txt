SECTION 1: OBJECTIVES, OWNERSHIP AND GOVERNANCE

1.1​ OBJECTIVES OF THE POLICY

The company is dedicated to upholding the highest standards of ethical
behaviour, which includes addressing all conflicts of interest responsibly.
Securitize manages regulated markets and commercial businesses that may
offer concerns of conflicts of interest. Conflicts of interest involve substantial
regulatory difficulties and may result in serious reputational harm if they are
not avoided or controlled.

Consequently, the principal objective of the Conflicts of Interest Policy is to
prevent situations of conflicts, ensure that Employees understand what a
conflict of interest is, and therefore, offer counsel to Employees on how to
manage such conflicts.

1.2​ SCOPE AND OWNERSHIP

1.2.1​ Scope

This policy applies to Securitize Europe Brokerage and Markets, S.V. S.A.
(“Securitize Europe”) and its majority-owned subsidiaries (collectively referred
to as the “Company”) and to all Securitize Europe employees including
consultants (among which interns and temporary staff) and agents of
Securitize Europe (collectively “You” or “Employees”).

The scope of this Policy is to:

   (i)​     Guarantee that conflict of interests is understood by Employees, in
            its many forms;
   (ii)​    To ensure prevention and avoidance of conflicts through the
            clarification of roles and responsibilities of Employees;
   (iii)​   Offer counsel to Employees on how to manage situations of
            interest of conflicts.

1.2.2​ Ownership

The owner of this policy is the Securitize Europe Brokerage and Markets, S.V.
S.A Managing Board. Compliance oversees the Policy and accompanying
paperwork up to date. The Policy should be evaluated and modified on an
annual basis in accordance with Securitize Europe group criteria.
1.3​ GOVERNANCE

1.3.1​   Responsibility and tasks of the Supervisory Board in connection with
this policy

The Company’s Audit Committee and/or the Chairman of the Supervisory
Board can evaluate and solve conflict specific situations escalated by
Compliance management.

1.3.2​ Responsibility and tasks of the Managing Board in connection with
this policy

The framework for managing conflicts of interest is overseen by the
Managing Board of Securitize Europe. This comprises organizational controls
and procedures for identifying, preventing, and managing conflicts of interest,
setting risk appetite boundaries regarding outside activities (see section 2.2.1
below), and approving policy revisions. Compliance oversees the day-to-day
implementation, monitoring, and maintenance.

1.3.3​ Reporting on this policy

Employees must report prospective, suspected, or real conflicts of interest to
the Compliance department for appropriate measures to be taken. If a
disclosed conflict cannot be resolved at the Compliance management level,
escalation to the Managing Board of Securitize Europe or, as appropriate,
under certain specific circumstances defined by Compliance, to the
Company's Audit Committee and/or Chairman of the Supervisory Board is
mandatory.

SECTION 2: DETAILED REQUIREMENTS

2.1​     DEFINITIONS AND ACTIONS CONSTITUTING CONFLICTS OF
INTERESTS

2.1.1​ Definitions and Actions

A “conflict of interest” is defined as a situation in which a person or business
or firm has an incentive to serve one interest at the expense of another
interest or obligation. It is not essential for such impairment to occur to
consider the existence of a conflict of interest; rather, it is sufficient that such
impairment may occur in the future. It is also insufficient to receive a benefit
if there is no potential disadvantage.

The Group will identify certain situations as conflicts of interest, including but
not limited to cases where any of its member companies, as well as their
directors, executives, employees, and affiliated representatives, or any
individual associated
with them through control, find themselves in any of the scenarios outlined
below, (and which will be furthered detailed):

   (i)​ At the expense of the client, they may acquire a financial benefit or
           avoid a financial loss.
   (ii)​ Has a vested interest in the outcome of a service given to the
           customer or a transaction carried out on the customer's behalf that
           differs from the customer's own interest in the outcome; or
   (iii)​Has financial or other incentives to prefer the interests of another
           client or group of clients over the client's interests;
   (iv)​Engages in the same professional activity as that of the client;
   (v)​ Receives or is about to receive an inducement in the form of money,
           goods, or services from a person other than the client in conjunction
           with a service rendered to the client, other than the customary
           commission or distribution for that service.

Such scenarios may include:

   (i)​       “Employee Conflict” an employee serving his own interests over
              that of the Company
   (ii)​      “Institutional Conflict” an employee favouring one Business Partner
              over others, or serving two Business Partners that have conflicting
              interests or,

   “Outside Activity” refers to any tasks that are carried out during official
   working hours, whether or not they are paid, such as serving on boards,
   holding board positions, engaging in external jobs or tasks, including
   standard teaching obligations or working for an organization, as well as
   any activity during non-office hours that could potentially pose a conflict
   of interest with the Company.

   “Office hours” are, in the context of this policy, refer to the hours stated in
   the employee's employment contract or communicated to them by Human
   Resources, except during notified holiday periods. If the specified office
   hours are not mentioned in the contract or a Human Resources
   notification, and only for enforcing the conflict-of-interest policy, office
   hours will be considered from 8 am to 8 pm, Monday to Friday, except for
   any notified holidays, which must be communicated by the employee to
   Human Resources.

   “Third-Party” refers to any third-party and/or external party to Securitize
   Europe, including for example:
(i)​   Any current or prospective client, customer, vendor, provider, or
       supplier of Securitize, such terms to be interpreted broadly to
       include
any person or entity that provides a service to the Company or from which the
Company obtains revenues.
    (ii)​    Any issuers, listed companies, market members, external market
             participants,
    (iii)​   Any Business Partner (as defined below),
    (iv)​    Any Public Official (as defined notably in the Anti-bribery Policy),
    (v)​     Any employee, representative, agent, intermediaries, or other
             individual associated directly or indirectly with the above,
    (vi)​    ​Any Securitize Europe Employees’ family members and relatives,
             irrespective such Third-Party is in a business relationship with the
             Company or not.

    “Business Partner” constitutes any Third-party falling into category 1. to 5.
    above.

    2.1.2​ General Principles

    Anyone subject to one of the general situations portrayed in 2.1.1 or the
    specific scenarios which apply to each category of individuals, must act in
    accordance with the following principles:

    (i)​     Independence: They must behave with independence of judgment,
             loyalty to the Group, and disregard for their own or outside interests
    (ii)​    Abstention: They must refrain from interfering with or influencing
             decision-making that may affect individuals or entities with whom
             they have a conflict, as well as from accessing confidential
             information that may have an impact on such conflict.
    (iii)​   Information: They must notify the Supervisory Body and the person
             in charge of the relevant area of any circumstances impacting them
             that may give rise to a conflict of interest.

    represented by certain situations which are considered inconsistent with
    the obligations of Employees.

    2.2.1​ Monitoring of Outside Activity

    and energies to the Company's business during normal office hours.

    Rules are as follows:

           no delay to both Compliance and Human Resources departments

(ii)​      During Office hours activities performed for the development of

           Human Resources Services.

In those situations where non-employment activities do not interfere with
the performance of their obligation to the Company and do not raise an
actual or potential conflicts of interest with the Company, Employees are
free to engage in activities during non-office hours. Those activities which
would constitute a conflict of interest situation should be communicated
to the Compliance Department to seek approval. Any doubt regarding the
potential constitution of a conflict of interest situation can be solved by
contacting Compliance.

2.2.2​Employees reporting obligations for selection process.

When an Employee is involved during the selection of a vendor, a supplier,
consultant or any other third party for business with the Company and
knows or has reason to believe that either he, a family member or anyone
with whom he has a financial or personal relationship may have a personal
(financial) interest in the outcome of the selection decision, must comply
with the notification requirements and must report to their manager and
Compliance.

2.3​INSTITUTIONAL CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

Because of its nature of market operator, the Company is potentially
subject to face conflict of interest with its Business Partners. Some
examples of the possible conflicts between the Company and its Business
Partners are provided to facilitate the identification by Employees:

-​ Important Business Partners such as members of its market centres
   and issuer admitted to listing/trading on its markets, may be the
   shareholders that own the Company;
-​ The Company is obligated in various countries in which it operates to
   perform regulatory functions and take disciplinary measures against
   its Business Partners, members or issuers in order to achieve its
   objective to operate tis markets, regulated or not in a fair and orderly
   manner. To achieve the same objective, the Company is also entitled to
   take disciplinary measures against its Business Partners, members or
   issuers;
-​ The Company may engage into contractual relationships with
   numerous business partners to provide similar products or services.
-​ Single Company's business leader may be in charge of competing
   businesses or other tasks.
-​ There may be sharing of customer information, trade data, or other
  knowledge between business units that should be kept separate;
-​ The Company may be instructed to advise, develop, or otherwise
  provide services to two different Business Partners that compete with
  one another, as there may be multiple relationships with one Business
  Partner.
When evaluating the risks that an Institutional Conflict presents,
Employees and manager should consider the severity of the risks, the
nature of the relationship with the affected Business Partner and in case if
the conflict may be inconsistent with the Company’s corporate strategy.

There are organizational controls and procedures in place to identify,
prevent and manage Institutional Conflicts of Interests such as:

(i)​     Administrative and Organizational Structure: The Company's
         various business units have been structured to minimize both
         potential conflicts within reporting lines and the flow of information
         among competing units. Senior management and the Managing
         Board are responsible for reviewing the Company's organizational
         structure on a regular basis, including when new business lines or
         types of activities are developed, in order to maintain a corporate
         governance structure that minimizes the potential for conflicts of
         interest among business units.


(ii)​    Information Barriers: In terms of business partner relationships, the
         Company follows a stringent confidentiality strategy. Employees
         whose roles include either business or regulatory/compliance
         functions involving members, issuers, or other business partners
         are responsible for keeping the information obtained during the
         course of their duties confidential. Employees can meet this
         requirement in a variety of ways, based on the functions done, the
         communications needed among business areas to perform those
         functions, and whether the information exists in hard or soft form.
                i.​ These means include restrictions on the use and content
                   of electronic communications; restrictions on access to
                   documents     and   office   areas;   information   security
                   measures designed to protect electronically stored
                   information; restrictions on communication of customer
                   information    among      specified    departments;     and
                   contractual provisions requiring confidentiality.
(iii)​   Disclosure to Business Partner or Regulator: If a conflict occurs in
         which a resolution would be detrimental to a Business Partner, the
         manager of the affected business unit is responsible for disclosing
         the conflict and resolution. The disclosure should be timely, clear,
         accurate, and comprehensive enough to allow the business partner
         to act in its own best interests. In addition, the manager, in
         collaboration with Compliance management and, if necessary, the
Legal Department, should evaluate whether disclosure to the
relevant regulator is necessary.
(iv)​    Contractual provisions: As previously stated, certain kinds of
         conflicts of interest between the Company and a Business Partner
         are possible. Contracts with Business Partners for such activities
         should state that if a specific conflict occurs, it will be handled in a
         specific manner or at the Company's discretion. Depending on the
         Business Partner, the head of the relevant business unit should
         collaborate with the Legal and Procurement Departments or the
         Vendor Management Office to ensure the inclusion of a suitable
         provision.
(v)​     Rulemaking disclosure that validates or resolves certain possible
         conflicts: The Company runs regulated markets, and subject to the
         authorization of the relevant authorities, the Company can
         implement rules that provide trading or other opportunities to
         members who access those markets. Members who meet certain
         criteria, for example, receive financial rebates for liquidity that they
         post in their allocated securities to execute against inbound orders,
         whereas other members do not.

SECTION 3: ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

3.1​ EMPLOYEES

All Employees are responsible for:

(i)​     Avoiding activities or relationships that may present an actual or
         potential conflict of interest;
(ii)​    Identifying and promptly disclosing a potential conflict of interest to
         their manager and Compliance Department;
(iii)​   Working cooperatively with their manager and Compliance to
         achieve a resolution;
(iv)​    Whenever there is a material change in relevant circumstances, a
         new clearance for previously approved activities has to be sought.

3.2​MANAGERS

Managers are responsible for:

(i)​     Making sure that appropriate and effective procedures are in place
         to prevent, identify, address or mitigate conflicts of interest;
(ii)​    Reviewing and take the appropriate measures with respect to a
         reported conflict in consultation with Compliance.
Informing and consulting with Compliance regarding potential conflicts of
interest and their problem or concern, as well as requesting Compliance's
input in determining a resolution.
3.3​COMPLIANCE STAFF MEMBERS

Compliance staff members are responsible for:

(i)​     Offering counsel relating to conflicts of interest to Employees
         including management;
(ii)​    Facilitating the determination of the appropriate resolution to the
         conflict or adequate measures that can be taken to mitigate the
         effects of conflict of interest that require Compliance review;
(iii)​   Elevating any conflicts that cannot be resolved at the level of
         Compliance staff member to Compliance management;
(iv)​    Maintaining in a log all reported conflicts of interest matters.

3.4​COMPLICANCE MANAGEMENT

Compliance Management is responsible for:

(i)​     Establishing the adequate measures that should be taken to reduce
         or resolve conflicts of interest that have been escalated to this level
         for review;
(ii)​    Monitoring the log of reported conflicts of interest on a quarterly
         basis and evidencing that review;
(iii)​   Escalating any reported conflicts that cannot be resolved at the
         Compliance Management level to the Managing Board of Securitize
         or, as appropriate, under certain specific circumstances determined
         by Compliance, to the Company’s Audit Committee and/or the
         Chairman of the Supervisory Board.

Where not forbidden by local laws and regulations, the information for
each conflict situation must include, at a minimum, the following:

(i)​     a description of the disclosed conflict;
(ii)​    the interrelated individuals and entities;
(iii)​   important dates during which the conflict arises and exists;
(iv)​    any related Company requirements; and
(v)​     the decision taken, the actions done to mitigate or resolve the
         conflict, and the identities of those involved in reaching the
         decision.

SECTION 4: DISCLOSURE AND ESCALATION

Employees who are unsure whether they are in a conflict of interest
situation should notify their manager and Compliance Department in
order for the previously mentioned procedures to be followed. The
preceding section is important for disclosure and escalation.
SECTION 5: RESOLUTION OF CONFLICTS

Resolutions​ may​ include​ some​ of​ the​ following,​ depending​ on​
the circumstances faced:

(i)​     approval of the underlying activity;
(ii)​    prohibition of engagement in an activity or entering into a business
         transaction;
(iii)​   removal of the Employee from a position of decision-making
         authority with respect to the conflict situation;
(iv)​    potential actions by the Company;
(v)​     suspension or termination of a vendor, consultant or other
         third-party contract; and
(vi)​    regulatory reporting.

SECTION 6: RETENTION OF RECORD OF CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

The log and any supporting documentation shall be retained for the periods
required in each jurisdiction in which the Company operates.
