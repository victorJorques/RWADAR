Volatility refers to price movements that occur over a specific period of time.
Specifically, in statistical terminology, volatility refers to the measure of
variability in a market or security's annualized returns during a specific
timeframe. It essentially quantifies the speed at which the price of the asset
rises or falls. Therefore, when the price of an asset experiences swift and
frequent fluctuations within a brief timeframe, reaching both higher and lower
points, it is considered to have high volatility. On the other hand, if the price
changes at a slower pace, either increasing or decreasing, or remains

relatively steady, it is described as having low volatility. The factors that
cause volatility are multiple, for instance, political and economic factors,
industry and sector factors or the performance of the company issuing the
financial instrument amongst others.

For this purpose, ESMA expects that a trading venue puts in place two types
of mechanisms (i) trading halts or price collars referenced to a static
reference price (static circuit breakers) and (ii) trading halts or price collars
referenced to a dynamic reference price (dynamic circuit breakers).

Each trading venue may adopt any of the above-mentioned mechanisms, as
long as it can effectively prove that it can control volatility with the
aforementioned mechanisms.

In this sense, and, taking as a reference the definition provided for by ESMA
in the Supervisory Briefing on the “Calibration of circuit breakers”, Securitize
shall establish trading halts in the form of mechanisms that interrupt
continuous trading and that cause trading to stope on a certain security for a
certain time period, in which no trades are executed and no new prices are
determined”.


Due to its close connection to volatility, this Circular also aims to clarify the
tick sizes settled for the Financial Instruments that will be negotiated in
Securitize, following the mandate of Commission Implementing Regulation
(EU) 2016/824 of 25 May 2016 laying down implementing technical
standards with regard to the content and format of the description of the
functioning of multilateral trading facilities and organized trading facilities
and the notification to the European Securities and Markets Authority
according to Directive 2014/65/EU of the European Parliament and of the
Council on markets in financial instruments (“Implementing Regulation (EU)
2016/824”).

The calculation of the ratio of unexecuted orders to transactions is also
specified in this Circular. Its importance lies on the fact that trading facilities
like the one operated by Securitize, operate high-speed systems, and as
orders are submitted, executed or canceled, the data is updated and
processed in real time. Therefore, the calculation allows tracking of the total
number of outstanding or unexecuted orders and the total number of
executed transactions. This ratio is fundamental to providing up-to-date
information on the current market conditions, liquidity, and trading activity.

SECTION 1: TRADING HALTS TO CONTROL VOLATILITY

The Market shall temporarily halt trading of a Negotiable Financial Instrument
if there is a significant fluctuation in its price in respect to the percentage
thresholds set out for the static and dynamic ranges to safeguard against
market volatility.

For the purposes of this section, the dynamic range will be defined as the
maximum variation allowed with respect to the last traded price of a
Negotiable Financial Instrument expressed in variation percentage (+/-).
Securitize will establish, review, adjust and publish on the Market Platform
(on an ongoing basis) the percentage thresholds for the dynamic range.

Additionally, for the purposes of this section, the static range will be defined
as the maximum variation allowed with respect to a static reference price of a
Negotiable Financial Instrument expressed in variation percentage (+/-).
Securitize will establish, review, adjust and publish on the Market Platform
(on an ongoing basis) the percentage thresholds for the static range. Each
time the dynamic or static range is exceeded during the trading session, a
trading halt will be activated and the trading for the Negotiable Financial
Instrument affected will be halted for as long as the Market considers
depending on the volatility of the prices. During the trading halt, it will be
possible to cancel and place Orders, but their execution will not be allowed.
Once the trading halt has elapsed, the new reference price for the Negotiable
Financial Instrument will be the price that triggered the dynamic range halt.

When a trading halt is activated, trading will be stopped for fifteen minutes,
according to Section 3 of this Circular.

Actions taken by the Securitize in accordance with the provisions set out in
this section will be publicly disseminated in the public announcement area,
the Market Member’s private area and via email to the Market Participants

SECTION 2: MONITORING CIRCUIT BREAKERS

Securitize shall have in place mechanisms to ensure that short-term volatility
episodes are adequately managed. For this purpose, Securitize shall trigger
each time the dynamic range is exceeded during the trading session. These
halts temporarily stop trading, providing market participants with time to
reflect on their assessment of fundamentals. .

SECTION 3: CALIBRATING CIRCUIT BREAKERS

Securitize shall calibrate its circuit breakers according to pre- defined,
statistically supported methodology. As such, Securitize shall consider the
following elements:

   a)​ the liquidity profile and the quotation level of the financial instrument;
   b)​ the volatility profile of the financial instrument;
   c)​   the internal references;
   d)​ the external references;
   e)​ the number of times the mechanism was used in the previous years on
       the platform.

When a trading halt is triggered, Securitize shall adjust the reference price
that triggered the halt.

For this purpose, Securitize shall determine the following:

         a)​ the price variation allowed by the dynamic and static thresholds;

         b)​ the dynamic and static reference price to be used;

         c)​ the frequency of their updates (In the case of the dynamic
             reference price, it will be updated intraday. The static reference
             price will be updated based on the last closing price of the previous
             trading session).

To calculate the applicable static and dynamic ranges, the following
information shall be considered:

SECTION 4: TRANSPARENCY REQUIREMENTS

Securitize shall make public its policies and arrangements in respect of
mechanisms to manage volatility by publishing the mentioned information on
its website on a clear and concise manner as well as the effects of trading
halts being triggered1.

This information should, as a minimum, include the following:

           a)​ a general description of the mechanism with possible scenarios in
               case a circuit breaker is triggered as well as explaining when the
               triggering would result in a trading halt;
           b)​ an explanation on the static and dynamic reference price used and
               the circumstances which would lead to the triggering of the
               mechanism;
           c)​ the duration of the halt should be indicated as well as an
               explanation on the price formation following trade resumption;
           d)​ statistics on the number of times the circuit breakers were
               triggered at least with an annual frequency.
           e)​ information on the level of thresholds that trigger the mechanism.

SECTION 5: REPORTING

As per Article 48(5) of MiFID II, Securitize shall annually provide to the CNMV
technical details regarding the functioning of the circuit breakers and
material changes that affect the implemented mechanism. These reports
should include relevant details such as the triggering events, duration of
trading halts, and any significant market developments during the halt period.

The smallest price movement or increment by which the price of a financial
instrument can fluctuate is referred to as the tick size of a market. More

precisely, the minimum price change in a financial instrument from which it
can be inferred that there is a price increment or decrement in the asset’s
value. Therefore, the tick size is relevant when identifying and predicting price
movements, being closely related to market volatility.

According to the Implementing Regulation (EU) 2016/824, multilateral trading
facilities like Securitize must provide to its competent authorities information
regarding the systems, procedures and arrangements to ensure compliance
with the conditions of Articles 48 and 49 of Directive 2014/65/EU (MiFID II
Regulation). Specifically, Article 49 refers to tick sizes and the need of
markets to adopt tick size regimes.

Specifically, in the case of Securitize, for Equity, the price tick will be
determined following the provisions set out in the Delegated Regulation (EU)
2017/588. For Debt Financial Instruments the price tick will be set at 0.01.

Regarding Equity, exchange traded funds where the underlying are equities
subject to the tick size regime or a basket of such equities, under the
provisions set out in the abovementioned Delegated Regulation, specifies
that the tick size that trading venues shall apply to orders in shares or
depositary receipts is to be equal to or greater than the one corresponding
to:

      (a)​ the liquidity band in the table in the Annex corresponding to average
         daily number of transactions in the most relevant market in terms of
         liquidity for that instrument; and


      (b)​the price range in that liquidity band corresponding to the price of the
         order.

For instance, whenever the daily number of transactions is below 10, and the
price ranges stands between 0 ≤ price < 0,1, the tick size will stand in 0,0005.

Regarding the tick size settled for Debt Financial Instruments at 0.01
percentage points, it means that the minimum price movement or increment
allowed for these instruments is 0.01 percentage points of the currency in
which they are denominated.

effectively entered in the Market Platform by each Market Member for every
Negotiable Financial Instrument traded.

in accordance with the methodology set out in article 3 of the Commission
